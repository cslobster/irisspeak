Data

Vocabulary, corpora, and training states

Everything the model learns from is derived from three public AAC corpora and a curated vocabulary. Each utterance is mapped to card ids and expanded into one training state per tapped-card position, with a soft target over the acceptable next cards.

Vocabulary

3,094 cards with stable ids, built from CBoard Classic, Project Core, a curated safety and repair set, and consensus across 25 AAC vocabularies, with per-category quotas. Multiword cards carry their component ids so the phrase and its expansion are both acceptable targets.

  • vocab.csv — one row per card: id, label, spoken form, aliases, category, intent, core and safety flags, composable, components
  • registry.json — append-only id registry with the version hash embedded in every model export
  • README.md and vocab_report.md — how it was built and the counts

Mapping and states

Longest-phrase-first matching over labels and aliases, lemma fallback, contraction handling, digits to number words, personal names to a name slot. Utterances are accepted at coverage of at least 0.85 and at most 6 cards. Acceptance: AACText 83.6 percent, Turk 61.1 percent, AAC Conversations 58.5 percent. Each accepted utterance expands into per-prefix states; the telegraphic variant and phrase expansions give a second acceptable next card where they diverge.

What the real data lacks: only about 1,800 real question-answer utterances have an AAC user answering, 1,113 of 3,094 cards never appear as a target, and the register is adult. The plan's quota-driven synthetic parent-and-child set addresses all three and has not been generated yet.
