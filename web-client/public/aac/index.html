The barrier isn't vocabulary size — it's the work between the thought and the word. Iris Speak removes the search.

What The Demo Just Demonstrated

  • Visual overwhelm. 84 targets at once. You are a literate adult and it still took effort — for a child with visual hyper-responsivity, this screen can trigger shutdown before communication begins.
  • Buried vocabulary. "Juice" isn't on the home page. The child must first learn that drinks live under an abstract category taxonomy — a cognitive prerequisite to speaking.
  • Wrong guesses are expensive. Open FOOD instead of DRINKS and you get 84 different cells to scan, none of them the word you wanted.
  • Navigation cost, not language cost. Most taps are spent getting to the word, not choosing it. Every tap is a chance to lose the thought.
  • Tiny motor targets. These cells are ~10 mm. Children with dyspraxia or tremor hit neighbors constantly — and one mis-tap can jump pages.
  • No context awareness. The grid is identical at breakfast, at the doctor, and mid-meltdown. It never helps.
